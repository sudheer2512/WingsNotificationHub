How to use this manual

This manual is for the people who run Wings Notification Hub from day to day through its administration console: administrators, template designers and approvers, campaign managers, operators and auditors. You do not need to be a developer.

It is written around tasks. Each task has a heading that starts with what you want to do, followed by numbered steps, what you will see, and what to do when something goes wrong.

  • New to the hub? Read to first.
  • Looking after messages today? Go to Part 2: covers finding, retrying and resolving deliveries.
  • Writing messages or automation? Go to Part 3, starting at .
  • Setting up a customer or provider? Go to Part 4, starting at .

Part 1

Getting started

What the hub does, how to sign in, how to find your way around, and what your role lets you do.

    What the hub does

    Wings Notification Hub is the one place your business applications go to send messages. An ERP, HRMS, CRM or other system hands the hub a request or a business event. The hub picks the wording, checks consent, chooses a provider, sends, retries and records exactly what happened.

    It carries seven channels: email, SMS, WhatsApp, in-app, push, web push and webhook. Customers can also reply on WhatsApp, SMS and email, and those replies arrive in the console's Inbox.

    A notification's journey

    ApplicationERP, HRMS or CRM sends a request or a business event
    Rules and templateA rule picks recipients and channels; a template becomes the message
    Consent checksPreferences, consent, suppressions and quiet hours
    ProviderRouting picks an account, sends, retries and falls back
    RecipientEmail, SMS, WhatsApp, in-app, push, web push or webhook
    Every step is recorded. Receipts from providers update the delivery's status, which you read in the Notification explorer and your application can receive by webhook.

    Key ideas

    TermWhat it means
    TenantA customer organisation on the hub. It has its own retention periods, consent setting and branding. Data never crosses between tenants.
    CompanyA business unit or legal entity inside a tenant, with its own time zone, quiet hours and branding.
    ApplicationA system that sends through the hub, such as Wings ERP Auto. It can send only for the companies it is enabled for.
    NotificationOne request to tell someone something, for example “invoice INV-10045 was created”.
    DeliveryOne message to one address on one channel. A single notification sent by email and WhatsApp has two deliveries, each with its own status.
    ChannelThe route a message takes: email, SMS, WhatsApp, in-app, push, web push or webhook.
    TemplateThe wording of a message for one channel and language, with variables such as {{CustomerName}}. Templates are versioned and approved by a second person.
    Notification typeA named kind of message, such as INVOICE_CREATED, with its message class, default channels and default template.
    RuleTurns a business event, such as payment.overdue, into notifications: who gets what, on which channel, under which conditions.
    ProviderThe outside service that carries a message, such as a mail server or SMS gateway. A provider account holds its settings and credentials.
    Sender identityThe from address, SMS sender id or WhatsApp number a message goes out under.
    Message classWhether a message is transactional, promotional or critical. See below.

    Message classes

    Every message has a class. The class decides which consent checks apply and whether quiet hours can delay it.

    ClassUse it forHow it behaves
    TransactionalMessages about something the recipient is party to: invoices, approvals, service updates.Honours the recipient's preferences, unless the notification type is mandatory. Ignores unsubscribes.
    PromotionalMarketing and offers.Needs marketing consent when the tenant requires it. Honours preferences and unsubscribes. Can be delayed by quiet hours.
    CriticalSecurity and regulatory notices.Ignores preferences and opt-outs. Never delayed by quiet hours. Still cannot reach a hard-bounced or invalid address, and WhatsApp still needs opt-in.

    Signing in

    You sign in with an email address and password from your administrator. Your session is kept only in the current browser tab, so a new tab asks you to sign in again.

    To sign in

    1. Open your hub's web address in a browser.
    2. Enter your Email and Password. Use the eye icon to show what you typed.
    3. Select Sign in. The console opens on the first screen your role allows, which is usually the Dashboard.

    To sign out

    1. Select your name at the top right of the console.
    2. Select Sign out.
    What you seeWhat it means and what to do
    Sign-in failed: Invalid email or password.Check both fields. If you still cannot get in, ask your administrator. The console has no “forgot password” link.
    Your session expired or was rejected. Sign in again.A session lasts 8 hours. It also ends when you sign out. Sign in again.
    No screens availableYour account has no roles. Ask an administrator to grant one ().

    Finding your way around

    1. Menu. Grouped into main screens, Operations and Administration. You see only what your role allows.
    2. Live indicator. Live with a green dot means updates arrive by themselves. Offline means the connection dropped; the console keeps retrying.
    3. Theme. Each click moves between system, light and dark.
    4. Live events bell. Failed deliveries and customer replies, as they happen.
    5. Your name. Your roles, theme choice and Sign out.
    6. API reference. The technical reference for developers.

    Using the live events bell

    A badge on the bell counts events you have not seen. Select the bell to list them, then select an entry to open that delivery or conversation. Only failures and outcomes that need a decision are listed, so routine progress does not crowd it out.

    • The list starts when you open the console and is kept only in that tab. For history, use the Notification explorer.
    • Turn Pop-ups for customer replies on or off at the top of the list. When it is on, a pop-up with an Open link appears at the bottom of the screen for each reply.

    What every screen has in common

    • Filters sit above the data. The screen updates as you change a select list. In a text box, press Enter.
    • Rows open things. Select a row in a table to see its detail, usually in a panel at the side or below.
    • Refresh reloads the data. Screens that show live data also update by themselves.
    • Times are shown in your browser's time zone. Exports from the explorer use UTC.
    • Errors appear in a red banner with a short message, the error code and a correlation id. Quote the correlation id when you ask for help.

    Choosing a light or dark theme

    Select the theme button in the top bar, or select your name and choose System theme, Light theme or Dark theme. System theme follows your computer's setting. The same menu shows your roles and scope.

    Using the console on a phone

    On a narrow screen the menu folds away behind the Menu button at the top left, cards stack, and wide tables scroll sideways inside their own area. Everything still works, but templates, rules and reports are easier on a larger screen.

    What each role sees

    The console shows each person only the screens and buttons their role allows. Roles are granted at a scope: a whole tenant, one company, one application or one channel.

    RoleTypical workNotable limits
    Platform administratorRuns the hub for all tenants: tenants, shared providers, credits and rates.None. The only role that can create tenants and change credits or rates.
    Tenant administratorRuns one tenant: companies, applications, users, providers, approvals.Cannot suspend its own tenant, add credits or change rates.
    Company administratorRuns one company: templates, rules, campaigns, providers, approvals.No Users & roles screen. Cannot edit tenants or companies, or manage credentials.
    Application administratorRuns one application: templates, rules, contacts, webhooks, credentials.No approvals, audit log or emergency suspension.
    Template designerWrites templates and notification types.Sees rules but cannot change them. Cannot approve. Cannot deliver a test of an active template.
    Template approverReviews, approves, rejects, rolls back and retires templates.Cannot edit template content. Sees only template requests in Approvals.
    Campaign managerBuilds campaigns, imports recipients, manages contacts.Cannot approve campaigns. Cannot use Test send on a campaign.
    Notification operatorWatches deliveries, retries, resolves, answers the Inbox, requeues dead letters.Sees providers and campaigns but cannot change them.
    AuditorReads everything, including the audit log and reports.Cannot change anything except acknowledging alerts. Never sees message bodies. Addresses are masked.
