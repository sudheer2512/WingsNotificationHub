Part 2

Daily work

Watch what is being sent, find a message, put right what went wrong, answer customers and decide approvals.

    Dashboard

    The dashboard answers one question at a glance: is messaging healthy right now?

    Narrowing what you see

    Use the filters across the top: Tenant (only if you can see more than one), Company, Application, Channel, Provider, From and To. The dashboard updates by itself when deliveries change, and at least once a minute.

    Reading the cards

    Selecting a card opens the Notification explorer filtered to that card, if your role can open the explorer. If it cannot, selecting a card does nothing.

    CardWhat it counts
    NotificationsNotification requests received. Hover to see the number of deliveries.
    Sent todaySent so far today.
    DeliveredConfirmed as delivered. Hover to see how many more were accepted by providers.
    FailedDeliveries that ended in failure.
    PendingScheduled, queued, processing, submitted or retrying.

    Reading the panels

    • Deliveries per hour (last 24 h) shows volume, with failures in a contrasting colour. peak is the busiest hour.
    • Channel usage shows deliveries per channel, then delivered / failed.
    • Provider health lists each provider's health, average response and attempts in 24 hours. Select Providers to open that screen.
    • Queues shows work waiting. A number under Dead-lettered needs attention: see .
    • Status breakdown counts deliveries per status. Select a status to open the explorer filtered to it.

    Notification explorer

    The explorer lists every delivery you are allowed to see. Use it to find out what happened to a message, and to put it right.

    Finding a delivery

    Who can do this: everyone who can open the explorer, which is every role except template designer and template approver.
    1. Open Notification explorer.
    2. Use any of the filters:
      • Company, Application, Channel, Provider, From, To, and Tenant if you see more than one.
      • Status, for example Failed.
      • Recipient (exact email or phone): the full address, such as customer@example.com or +919876543210. Part of an address finds nothing.
      • Notification type, such as PAYMENT_OVERDUE. Lower case also works.
      • Notification id, if someone gave you one.
      • Awaiting operator, to list only deliveries that need a decision.
    3. Press Enter after typing in a text box. The total at the top shows how many match.
    4. Select Date / time, Attempts or Cost in the table heading to sort.

    Leave Live switched on and the first page reloads when statuses change. Switch it off to hold the list still while you read.

    Reading a delivery

    Select a row. A panel opens on the right with the delivery's details and three tabs.

    1. Retry appears when a retry is possible.
    2. Failure shows the error code and reason.
    3. Timeline: every event in order.
    4. Attempts: each try at a provider.
    5. Content: the message as rendered.
    • The details list shows the recipient, type and template with its version, the source of the request, provider, attempts, cost and any failure. Links such as Fallback of open the related delivery.
    • Timeline shows who or what changed the status. source: tells you whether it came from the hub, the provider or an operator.
    • Attempts shows the outcome of each try, the response code, the time taken and the provider's sanitised response.
    • Content shows the rendered message. Content hidden means your role cannot read message bodies. Content purged means the retention period has passed.
    • Full history lists all deliveries of the same notification, with attempts and events counted. Addresses are masked in this list.
    Attempt outcomeWhat it means
    AcceptedThe provider took the message.
    Transient failureThe provider did not take it, and trying again may work. The hub retries or falls back by itself.
    Permanent failureThe provider refused it for good, for example a bad address. Retrying will not help until the cause is fixed.
    AmbiguousNobody knows whether the provider took it, for example a timeout after sending. See .
    ThrottledThe provider asked the hub to slow down. This does not count as a failed attempt.

    What each status means

    A delivery normally moves through Queued Processing Submitted Provider accepted Sent Delivered Read. Not every channel reaches every status: some email and SMS providers never confirm delivery, and only some channels report that a message was read.

    Needs attentionWhat to do
    FailedRead the failure, fix the cause, then retry ().
    UnknownDecide with Resolve ().
    ExpiredIt ran out of time before it could be sent. Retry if it is still useful.
    SuppressedThe hub chose not to send it. Read the reason on the Timeline, then see . Do not retry.
    Retry pendingThe hub will try again by itself. You can also select Retry.

    All statuses are in .

    Retrying a failed delivery

    Who can do this: platform, tenant, company and application administrators, and notification operators.
    1. Find the delivery and open it. Retry appears for Failed, Expired and Retry pending.
    2. Read the Failure line. If the address is wrong or blocked, fix that first, or the retry will fail the same way.
    3. Select Retry. You see Retry scheduled (RetryPending). Earlier attempts stay on the timeline.
    4. Watch the panel: the status and Timeline update by themselves.

    A retry keeps the same delivery. Earlier attempts stay on the timeline as a permanent record, and a new attempt is added below them.

    Resolving an unknown outcome

    Sometimes a connection fails just after a message was handed over, so nobody knows whether the provider took it. The hub will not resend by itself when that could send a duplicate. The delivery stops at Unknown, is marked Awaiting operator, and raises a warning alert.

    Who can do this: platform, tenant, company and application administrators, and notification operators.
    1. On the Dashboard, select the Awaiting operator card. Or tick Awaiting operator in the explorer.
    2. Open the delivery.
    3. Check with the provider or the recipient whether the message arrived. The Provider line and the Attempts tab give you the reference to quote.
    4. Select Resolve and choose:
      • Resend (accept duplicate risk): it did not arrive, or a duplicate does no harm. The delivery is sent again.
      • Mark as sent: the provider did accept it. Nothing is resent.
      • Mark as failed: it did not go, and you do not want to resend. Nothing is resent.
    5. Enter a Comment for the audit trail, then select Resend or Confirm.

    Resending an invoice twice is usually harmless. Resending a payment reminder or a one-time code may not be. When in doubt, check with the provider first.

    Exporting to a file

    1. Set the filters. The export contains what the filters select.
    2. Select Export, then CSV or Excel (.xlsx). The file downloads with a name such as deliveries-20261106-1406.csv.

    Email addresses and phone numbers are masked in exports, for every role. Times in the file are UTC, so they differ from the local times on the screen.

    Inbox

    When customers reply on WhatsApp, SMS or email, their messages arrive here as conversations, and you can answer from the console.

    Reading conversations

    Who can do this: everyone who can open the Inbox. Replying needs a role with inbox rights: platform, tenant and company administrators, and notification operators. Auditors and application administrators read only.
    1. Open Inbox. Conversations are on the left, most recent first. A number shows unread messages.
    2. Narrow the list with Search address or name (press Enter), Channel or Unread only.
    3. Select a conversation. It is marked as read if your role can reply.

    Type names exactly as they appear, including capitals. In the demo system Ramesh finds a conversation and ramesh does not. A phone number or part of one always works.

    Each conversation shows its routing to your application: Routed means the application received the reply, Pending means it has not yet, and No subscriber means no application is listening for replies (see ).

    Replying to a customer

    1. Open the conversation.
    2. Type in Reply, up to 4096 characters.
    3. Select Send reply, or press Ctrl+Enter (Cmd+Enter on a Mac). You see Reply queued, and the message appears with its status.
    4. Select details under a message to open its delivery in the explorer.

    The WhatsApp 24-hour window

    WhatsApp allows free-form replies only within 24 hours of the customer's last message. The conversation heading counts down, for example Window closes in 23 h 26 min.

    After that it shows Window closed, and the reply box is turned off. Send an approved WhatsApp template instead, or wait for the customer to write again.

    A reply is added to a conversation only when the hub can match it to a notification or customer. A message on a shared provider that cannot be matched does not appear in the Inbox. It raises a warning alert on System monitoring instead.

    Platform administrators also see Simulate reply. It is a test helper and works only on a test or development hub.

    Approvals

    Important changes need a second person. This is called maker-checker: whoever asked for the change cannot approve it.

    KindWhat approving doesWho can decide
    Template versionActivates the version. New notifications use it at once.Template approver, company, tenant and platform administrators
    CampaignSchedules the campaign, or starts it if it has no schedule.Company, tenant and platform administrators
    Provider credentialsReplaces the stored credentials of a provider account.Company, tenant and platform administrators
    AI notificationSends a message that an AI agent asked to send.Company, tenant and platform administrators

    A template approver sees only template versions on this screen. Other roles see the kinds they can decide.

    Approving or rejecting a request

    1. Open Approvals. Pending is selected.
    2. Select open under a request to review it first. Each kind opens the screen where you can read the detail.
    3. Select Approve or Reject.
    4. Add a comment. It is optional when you approve, and a Reason is required when you reject.
    5. Confirm. The request leaves the list.

    To review past decisions, select Approved, Rejected or Cancelled. A Decision column shows the outcome, when it happened and the comment.

    AI agents can send only notification types that allow it. If a type also needs human approval, the agent's request waits here until someone decides. Rejecting tells the requester through the audit trail. No message goes to the recipient.
